## Questions Asked (Full Text)

**Q1 — On Difficult Conversations and Follow-Through**
> Think about the last time you had a genuinely difficult conversation with someone on your team — something where you had to push back hard, address a performance gap, or confront a pattern that wasn't changing. My question is: did that conversation end with a specific, concrete next step — who would do what, by when?
> And if it did end that way, did you actually follow up three weeks later to see if the thing happened?

**Answer:** Yes, I had a performance related discussion with a team member. The member is being put on a success plan due to a performance gap last year, I had to explain what is it in an objective way, make sure that he is not demotivated, clearly articulate the expectations, and what the plan really meant. I'll be following up on that with him for the next few weeks.

---

**Q2 — On the Difference Between Talking and Solving**
> When you give someone critical feedback — not a suggestion, but something that requires them to actually change — what's your internal metric for whether the conversation was a success? Is it: they understood you? They agreed with you? They said they'd change? Or is it something further downstream?

**Answer:** Mostly: whether they are able to understand my context - where I'm coming from. It'll be a failure if they didn't get what they need to change and *why*.

---

**Q3 — On Relationship Maintenance in Practice**
> You have a team that's been doing significant, high-scope work on Agentic AI. I'm curious: in the last month, what's the single most relationship-oriented thing you've done for someone on your team — not task-related, not a review or a check-in, but something that was purely about the relationship?
> And if it was more than two weeks ago, what happened to the space for that?

**Answer:** Actually cannot think of one.

---

**Q4 — On the Cost of "Always Something More Urgent"**
> You've named in previous sessions that there are always many things competing for your attention. Here's the specific version I want to probe: think of one person on your team right now who is probably carrying more than their share of the invisible work — the stuff that keeps the system running but doesn't show up in status updates. What have you done in the last two weeks to make sure they feel seen and not just used?

**Answer:** I'm planning to create a template for weekly async status updates with a few called-out sections (e.g. customer escalations which often take up more than necessary engineer's time). I'll also add a Misc section as catch all. This page will be a source for people to provide what took most of their time on a weekly basis.

---

**Q5 — On What "Being Accessible" Actually Means**
> You lead a team where you're visible, high-trust, and probably seen as approachable. Here's the harder question: when was the last time you were genuinely curious about someone's situation — not because it was blocking a project, not because it was your job, but because you wanted to understand how they were actually doing? What did you learn?

**Answer:** Yeah, I do that in my 1:1s. For example, in last week's 1:1 with Hessam I got to know that Zia was given a hard time by some other support teams, and I used that in the next 1:1 with Zia to appreciate his efforts. But it's a good reminder to ask people more stuffs beyond work which I don't get to do often.

---

**Q6 — On the Pattern of Deferred Relationship Work**
> One of the things that separates managers who build lasting teams from those who build capable-but-fragile ones is whether the team would describe their leader as "someone who shows up for me even when nothing is on fire." What would have to be true for that to be an accurate description of you — not aspirationally, but actually?

**Answer:** I want to build a culture of appreciation where team members regularly recognize efforts from others. I plan to have a Friday thanks train where in the weekly report itself there would be a section for thanking anyone who went above and beyond.
