## Research Synthesis

**On Proportionality:**
Great engineering managers calibrate their emotional response to the actual severity of the issue. Research on leadership emotional regulation shows that disproportionate reactions — treating a minor miss as a crisis — erode trust over time. Engineers stop bringing early warnings because they learned the leader will catastrophize. The failure mode is especially dangerous for Senior EM who carries organizational visibility: when you react large, your team feels they must react large. What looks like "high standards" is often just noise.

Key insight: The leader's reaction sets the team's calibration for what constitutes a real emergency. Outsize reactions create a culture of escalating until the leader's stress levels become the threshold for action.

**On Learning Board:**
The "many mentors, not one" model is especially relevant at ServiceNow scale. The recommendation is to diversify across dimensions: craft, strategy, character, industry, life. What makes this powerful for tech leaders is that different mentors give you permission to think in different time horizons. Your craft mentor might be a principal engineer thinking quarter-level; your strategy mentor might be a director thinking 18 months out; your character mentor might be someone who's navigated a similar career arc before.

Key insight: The failure mode is seeking one perfect mentor — which usually means no mentor, because the perfect one doesn't exist.

## Questions Delivered

**Q1 — On personal calibration:**
> You've been at ServiceNow for a meaningful stretch now, running Agentic AI work at significant scope. There's a pattern I want to probe: when something goes wrong — a demo stumbles, a milestone slips, an engineer makes a visible mistake — how do you typically first register it internally?
>
> Specifically: do you notice your body reacting before your thinking catches up? And when you catch yourself reacting, do you find you can recalibrate, or does the initial charge carry through?

**Q2 — On team signals (proportionality):**
> Think about your current team — the engineers who are doing the core work on Agentic AI. When one of them makes a mistake that's visible to others — maybe it breaks something in a demo, maybe it's a wrong call in a design document — how do you show up in that moment?
>
> More specifically: after the immediate issue is resolved, do your team members tend to feel like they can bring you the next problem early, or do they wait until it's safe?

**Q3 — On mentorship portfolio (learning board):**
> Who are the five people — current or former — you'd go to if you needed to think through a genuinely hard career move? Not advice on tactics, but people who help you see clearly.
>
> And second: is there a gap in that group? Someone you'd benefit from having who isn't currently in your orbit?
